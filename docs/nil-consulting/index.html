Know the rules

The rules in 60 seconds

Plain-English summaries, not legal advice. Rules change, so check with your school's compliance office before you sign anything.

High school in Florida

FHSAA Bylaw 9.9

Endorsements and social media are allowed. The agreement must be in writing and end by graduation. No school logos or uniforms, no banned categories (alcohol, vaping and gambling among them) and no using NIL to recruit. The student and parent file an Affidavit of Compliance with the school within five business days of signing.

Source: FHSAA Handbook 2024-25, revised Feb. 2025 (opens in a new tab)

College, Division I

NIL Go

Third-party deals worth $600 or more must be reported to NIL Go within five business days. Each deal needs a valid business purpose: promoting a product or service sold to the public.

Source: College Sports Commission (opens in a new tab)

Questions

NIL questions, STRAIGHT answers

Are you an NIL agent?

No. I'm not a licensed athlete agent, and I don't represent athletes, negotiate deals or take a commission. In Florida, representing a college athlete on NIL contracts takes an athlete agent license.

Can you get my athlete a deal?

No. I teach the content and pitching skills. The deals are the athlete's, and every one should go past the school's compliance office before anyone signs.

Can Florida high school athletes do NIL?

Yes, under FHSAA Bylaw 9.9, with limits: a written agreement that ends at graduation, no school logos or uniforms, no banned categories, no recruiting use, and an affidavit filed with the school within five business days of signing. More in Florida NIL rules for high school athletes.

Do free products need a disclosure?

Yes. Under the FTC Endorsement Guides, free or discounted product is a material connection, so say so clearly, in the video itself. “#ad” works. “Collab” or “ambassador” alone does not.

Is NIL money taxable?

NIL income is reportable income. Ask a tax professional how that applies to you, including any free product you receive.
