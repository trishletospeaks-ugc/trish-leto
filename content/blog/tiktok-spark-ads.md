## What TikTok Spark Ads are

**TikTok Spark Ads** are TikTok's own format for putting a real organic post behind ad spend. TikTok's help center calls Spark Ads "a native ad format" that lets advertisers use organic TikTok posts and their features in their advertising ([TikTok Ads Manager Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/spark-ads)). Those posts can be the brand's own, or posts made by other creators, with their authorization.

That second part is where I come in. I'm Trish Leto, a GenX UGC creator, model and Navy veteran in Tampa, and I make short videos for brands: talking heads, "yap" style, lifestyle, product demos and b-roll. When a brand wants a creator's video to show up in the For You feed as an ad, and not just sit on a product page, Spark Ads is TikTok's built-in way to do it.

Here's the short version, in plain English:

- A Spark Ad looks and behaves like a regular TikTok post, because it is one.
- The post can come from the brand's own account or from a creator's account.
- A creator's post needs the creator's permission, and that permission comes as a code.

## How the Spark Ads code works (as of October 2026)

TikTok updates its menus often, so treat these steps as a snapshot of its help pages as of October 2026. If a button has moved, the help pages win.

### The creator's side

Per TikTok's help page on [creating Spark Ads for Smart+ campaigns](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-create-spark-ads-for-smart-campaigns-on-tiktok-ads-manager), the creator:

1. Allows authorized posts in the app, under Settings and privacy, then Privacy (or under Creator tools).
2. Opens the post, taps the three dots and taps **Ad settings**.
3. Agrees to TikTok's advertising content terms and turns on the **Ad authorization** toggle.
4. Generates a video code and picks how long it lasts: **7, 30, 60 or 365 days**.
5. Taps **Copy code** and sends it to the brand.

TikTok's [Spark Ads creation guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/spark-ads-creation-guide) describes the same short path: go to the video, tap the three dots, tap Ad settings, then tap Generate to get the post code. That code is what people mean when they say "send me your **Spark Ads code**."

### The brand's side

Once the brand has the code, the same Smart+ page says to go to **Tools**, then **Creative library**, then **Spark Ads posts**, click **Apply for Authorization**, paste the code into the search bar, search, and confirm the post. From there, the authorized post can be picked as the creative when the ad is built.

Two details from TikTok's creation guide are worth knowing before anyone hits publish:

- **The caption is locked.** TikTok says you can't edit a post's caption after it's been authorized as an ad, so agree on the final caption first.
- **Private becomes public.** A private video becomes public once it's used in a campaign, and its privacy setting can't be changed during the promotion.

Duets and Stitches are a special case. TikTok's Smart+ page says a post that features two creators needs a video code from both of them.

## Spark Ads vs whitelisting vs Partnership Ads

"Whitelisting" is a word you'll hear a lot in creator deals, and it's fuzzy. It's industry shorthand for running ads through a creator's account. You won't find a button called whitelisting in either ads platform. On TikTok, the official format is Spark Ads. On Facebook and Instagram, it's **Partnership Ads**. In May 2025, Meta said that where Partnership Ads used to list both the advertiser and a partner in the ad's header, advertisers can now feature just one partner in the header on Facebook and Instagram ([Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/meta-at-iab-newfronts-2025)).

## Why brands and creators both like Spark Ads

The big structural difference is where the engagement lands. TikTok's [About Spark Ads page](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/spark-ads) says all of the engagement on a Spark Ads post is attributed to the original organic post. The likes, comments and shares the ad earns pile up on the real post, not on a separate ad copy.

For a brand, that means the ad carries its social proof with it and looks native, because it is native. For a creator, it means the post keeps what the ad budget brings in. That's a fair trade, as long as the paperwork is clear about which post, for how long, and for what.

## Disclosure still applies to Spark Ads

Putting ad spend behind a post doesn't make its disclosure duties go away. TikTok's [Branded Content Policy](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/bc-policy/en) defines branded content as content that promotes or reviews a third-party brand or its products or services "in exchange for payment or any other incentive." "Any other incentive" is broad. The policy also has creators turn on TikTok's content disclosure setting for branded content, and it makes the creator responsible for following the law.

The FTC is just as clear about video. Its [Disclosures 101 guide for influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) says the disclosure should be in the video, not just in the description uploaded with it. So the hook can be playful, but the disclosure can't be hiding.

And for the record: a product showing up in one of my own videos is my content. It is not proof that the brand hired me, and I won't present it that way. This section is general information, not legal advice.

## Spark Ads rights and pricing: what to agree on

A Spark Ads code is permission for one post, for a set number of days. A good agreement spells out everything around it. Here's the checklist I'd hand any brand:

- **Which posts.** List each video by link.
- **How long.** 7, 30, 60 or 365 days. Heads up: TikTok says Spark authorization requests sent through its Content Suite default to 365 days ([TikTok Ads Manager Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-use-content-suite-to-create-and-launch-spark-ads)). If the deal is 30 days, write 30 days.
- **Where else it runs.** TikTok only, or Meta Partnership Ads and the brand's own uploads too.
- **The final caption.** It's locked once the post is authorized.
- **Disclosure.** Who turns on the content disclosure setting, and what the in-video disclosure says.
- **Raw files.** Whether the brand also gets the clean video and b-roll for its own ads.

On price, I'm not going to throw a number at you here, because every package I put together is a custom quote. What I can share is how the market talks about it. One vendor pricing guide on [ppc.io](https://ppc.io/blog/ugc-pricing), a vendor blog and not a study, says some brands pay an extra 30% to 100% of the base fee per month for permission to run ads through the creator's account. Treat that as a rough range you'll hear quoted, not a rate card.
